About one in five enquiries that come to us start with "we need armed guards." About one in twenty actually does. The remaining four out of five would be better served by a professional unarmed deployment, ideally combined with one or two other measures. This post is the framework we walk every prospect through.

## The cost gap

Armed guards in this region cost ₹25,000–₹35,000 per month per guard, compared with ₹10,000–₹18,000 for unarmed. That's roughly **2.5× the cost**, before factoring in the additional licensing, weapons maintenance, and qualification drills. Over a 24-month contract with 5 guards, that's a difference of roughly ₹18 lakh.

That gap has to be justified by an actual threat profile. Otherwise you're not buying more safety — you're buying a more expensive feeling of safety.

## When armed guards are genuinely required

There are three categories of site where armed deployment is the right call:

### 1. Cash, bullion and valuables handling
- Bank branch security (especially the cash chest)
- ATM cash replenishment escort
- Jewellery showrooms above a certain stock value
- Cash-in-transit (CIT) operations
- Bullion vault security

The threat is real, frequent enough to justify the premium, and the response window measured in seconds rather than minutes.

### 2. High-value and high-symbolism targets
- Petroleum / gas storage facilities (regulatory armed-guard requirement)
- High-tension power substations in remote locations
- Telecom tower sites in areas with reported diesel theft and operator threats
- Critical-infrastructure perimeters

### 3. Specific, credible, named threats
- A client who has received a documented threat (extortion, intimidation)
- A senior executive with a specific protection requirement
- An asset that has recently been the subject of an attempted incident

For these three categories, armed deployment is appropriate. We'd recommend it ourselves.

## When armed guards are usually not required

In our experience, the following enquiries usually start with "we want armed guards" and end with an unarmed deployment that solves the actual problem better:

- **Corporate office building** — almost never needs armed. Access control, well-trained unarmed guards, CCTV and a quick-response patrol arrangement give you better real-world security than a single armed guard at the gate.
- **Standard residential housing society** — armed deployment is illegal in most residential contexts without specific justification.
- **Schools and colleges** — armed presence around children sends the wrong message and is operationally counter-productive. Trained unarmed guards with strong visitor management is the right answer.
- **Retail stores** (other than jewellery) — armed is overkill; trained unarmed staff with anti-shoplifting protocols are more effective.
- **Hotels and resorts** — guest experience is the priority; armed presence is jarring and rarely justified.
- **Hospitals** — patient-facing roles need de-escalation skills more than they need weapons.

## What armed deployment actually requires

If your site genuinely is in the "armed needed" category, here's what you should expect from your agency:

- Licensed weapons — agency licence is not enough; each weapon has its own licence
- Trained personnel — typically ex-servicemen or retired police. PSARA prescribes additional training for armed personnel.
- Quarterly weapons handling and qualification drills
- A clear escalation chain — armed personnel must never be the only line of response; they're a deterrent and a last resort
- Insurance specifically covering armed deployment
- Written rules of engagement in the contract

Anyone offering "armed guards" without all of the above is selling you a uniform and a liability, not security.

## The middle path: unarmed + quick response

Most sites that think they need armed deployment are actually better served by a hybrid:

- Trained unarmed guards on post (the routine deterrent and reporting layer)
- A nearby quick-response patrol team that can reach the site in 5–15 minutes (the escalation layer)
- Strong CCTV with monitored review (the evidence and detection layer)

This costs significantly less than full armed coverage and provides better outcomes in most threat scenarios. We deploy this model across many of our corporate and industrial clients.
