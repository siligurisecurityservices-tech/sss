A real-estate project goes through three distinct phases, each with a different security problem. The mistake most developers make is deploying the same generic guard count across all three. The phases need different deployments — and the right deployment in each phase saves money in the phase that follows.

## Phase 1: Construction (months 0–24)

**The problem:** Material theft (rebar, cement bags, electrical fittings, copper wire), unauthorised labour intrusion, vehicle access control, theft of contractor equipment overnight.

**Typical losses on an unsecured 5-acre construction site:** Material shrinkage of 4–8% of project budget — at typical Siliguri project sizes, that's ₹15–40 lakh you simply lose to theft over the project lifetime.

**Deployment model we recommend:**
- 2 guards day shift (gate + materials in/out register)
- 3 guards night shift (gate + perimeter patrol × 2)
- Supervisor visits 3× per week
- Visible signage, materials register signed by the contractor's storekeeper, photo records of inbound and outbound

**Monthly cost:** ~₹70,000–95,000 (Standard tier, 5 guards on rotation)
**Typical saving:** Pays for itself in month 2 in most projects.

## Phase 2: Sales gallery / sample flat (months 12–30)

**The problem:** Sample flat is unattended after-hours, has expensive fixtures and finishes that get pilfered, location is now a high-traffic public-facing site with hundreds of visitors weekly. Security here is half about asset protection and half about brand presentation.

**Deployment model:**
- 1 well-groomed guard at the sample-flat entrance during opening hours
- 1 night guard for the sample flat and sales gallery
- Female guard during women's-only viewing slots if you offer them

**Monthly cost:** ~₹35,000 (Premium tier — the customer-facing presentation matters more than for construction sites)

## Phase 3: Post-handover society security (years 1+)

**The problem:** Now you're running a residential society. Visitor management, parking enforcement, vendor entry, club-house and amenities security, occasional handover-period disputes between developer and residents.

**Deployment model varies by society size**, but a typical 200-flat society in Siliguri runs:
- 2 guards day, 2 guards night at main gate
- 1 floating patrol guard
- 1 club-house / amenities guard during operating hours
- Optional: 1 supervisor present 8 hours daily

**Monthly cost:** ~₹90,000–₹1.3 lakh depending on amenities scope

## The handover trap

The expensive mistake is over-deploying in Phase 1 (more guards than the construction site needs) and under-deploying in Phase 3 (because the developer doesn't want to set a precedent that the AOA / RWA finds expensive to maintain after handover).

Get this backwards and:
- Phase 1: You're paying for guards you don't need; theft happens anyway because the deployment isn't structured around the actual risk vectors.
- Phase 3: You hand over a society with under-staffed security; the first major incident happens in month 2 post-handover; AOA blames the developer; brand damage for your next project in the same city.

## What our developer clients ask us to do differently

Across our developer clients in Siliguri, here are the three things we are now asked to provide that we weren't 5 years ago:

1. **A monthly security report**, not just an invoice. Photo log, incidents, materials register summary, anomalies. Goes to the project director's email automatically.
2. **A "handover SOP" deliverable** that the AOA receives at handover, with our SOPs, escalation contacts and a 30-day transition plan. AOA can choose to retain us, change provider, or modify scope — but they inherit a documented operation, not chaos.
3. **Flexibility in the contract** to scale deployment up and down between phases without renegotiating the whole agreement. We build this into our base contract for developer clients by default now.
